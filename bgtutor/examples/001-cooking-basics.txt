Host: Welcome back to Kitchen Stories. I'm Anna, and today I'm in the kitchen with chef Georgi, who is going to show us how to make banitsa, probably the most famous Bulgarian pastry. Georgi, thanks for coming.
Guest: Thank you for having me. Banitsa is something every Bulgarian grandmother makes, and every family thinks theirs is the best. So I'll try not to start an argument today.
Host: Let's start simple. What do we need?
Guest: Not much. A pack of thin filo pastry, four eggs, about two hundred grams of white brined cheese, a cup of yoghurt, a little baking soda and some butter or oil. That's it. The cheese is the important part. In Bulgaria we call it sirene, and it's a bit like feta.
Host: And how do you make it?
Guest: First you mix the eggs, the yoghurt and the crumbled cheese in a bowl. Then you take two sheets of pastry, brush them with butter, spread some filling on top and roll them up loosely. You put the rolls in a round tray, one after another, like a spiral. When the tray is full, you pour the rest of the filling over the top and bake it for about forty minutes at one hundred and eighty degrees.
Host: That smells amazing already. When do people usually eat it?
Guest: Mostly for breakfast, with a glass of ayran or boza. But on New Year's Eve banitsa is special. We hide little notes with wishes inside, called kasmeti, and everyone gets a piece. Whatever your note says, health, love, a trip, that's your luck for the new year.
Host: I love that. Georgi, thank you. Next week we'll try something sweet.
